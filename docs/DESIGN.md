# HEARTHFALL — Master Design Document (Revision 2)

*Revised against three critiques (mystery, puzzle integrity, feasibility). Every change is folded into the body; Appendix A lists the few critiques rejected or modified, with reasons; Appendix B is the flag register implementers need. Numbers in this document (routes, vote uniqueness, Hymn-contour uniqueness, ring rotation, shelf positions) were checked by brute force during revision.*

## 1. Title, logline, tone, art, sound

**Title:** HEARTHFALL

**Logline:** Four fourth-year Wardens of Thornhallow, each with a different way of seeing, must smuggle their friend Wren — the prophesied "one born of four" — through one night of politics, wards and bells to the Cold beneath their school, and decide, when the Hearth goes cold, who really has to walk in.

**Tone:** Warm and funny at the table, cold underneath. Hogwarts-at-midnight in the first hour; Winterfell politics in the council; Myst silence in the vault; a Detroit finale where four friends find out what each of them was carrying. The game never mocks the players' affection for Wren; that affection is the whole engine — and, it turns out, the whole mechanism.

**Art direction (SVG/CSS/canvas only):**
- The Hearth screen is dominated by a procedural fire (canvas particles + layered SVG flame paths) that is the night's health bar: full at KINDLE, a blue tongue by Chapter 5, a spark in the Finale. It is also, secretly, a curtain: the foot of the prophecy stone above it is hidden by flame until Chapter 6.
- Scenes are three layered SVG planes with slow CSS keyframe drift (no mouse parallax — nobody at a TV has a mouse over the scene), ink-and-wash: slate, soot, ember-orange, one cold blue for the Cold. Characters are silhouettes with lit edges; faces are never drawn, so text carries expression.
- **The Map of the Night:** a vellum chart at the top of the Hearth that unfolds a panel per chapter (paper-crease animation): dormitory → Great Hall → Vault → Gallery → Study → the Long Stair, then the sheet **turns over** in Chapter 5 to reveal the Under-Marches beneath the school — the world is a lid. A thin gold line records the party's route all night.
- Phones: black slate. Each Sighting has a colour (Reader amber, Listener teal, Seer violet, Binder red). The Seer's under-layer pages are white blueprint lines on black. In every under-layer that contains Wren and a flame, the four players' shadows are drawn falling **away** from the fire and Wren's falling **toward** it, in the same frame, from the Prologue on.
- Flowchart: Detroit-style vertical chart; taken nodes lit, untaken nodes grey with "?", private nodes as sealed boxes labelled by nickname ("only the Listener knows").

**Sound direction (WebAudio, no samples, no speech):**
- Hearth drone: low sine + band-passed noise crackle whose density tracks the flame.
- The Founders' Tongue is a seven-note ladder plus a rest: ASH (step 0), THORN (1), KNOT (2), EMBER (3), WELL (4), VEIL (5), CROWN (6), **COLD = a rest**. Every glyph clicked on the Hearth plays its note (bell FM synth). The Founders' Hymn is the eight glyphs in wall order and ends on a rest.
- **Bells are tuned to the room.** The Listener's phone never plays absolute pitch: it plays each room's phrase as *steps* ("up one, up three, down two, then a rest") with a matching arrow strip, and offers a "row-player" that plays any ordering of glyphs the Listener drags together so they can test it against the room by ear *or* by arrows. Nothing in the game requires pitch-by-ear.
- "Four Hands" (all keys within 1 s) plays a four-note kindle chord; in the Finale each key sustains one note.
- Bells: FM bell per lane, detuned per player colour. Heartbeats (Listener only): filtered pulse. Frost: high shimmer noise.
- All dialogue is text with a per-character "voice tick" (short synth blip). No speechSynthesis anywhere.
- Phone audio only plays after a tap ("cup your ear") for iOS; every audio page has a waveform/arrow strip and, via the hint bell, a text fallback.

## 2. Platform and session flow

**The Hearth (laptop/TV):** story scenes, the Map, the four interactive components (§2, *Components*), group choices with countdown rings (always announced 5 s before they start), **the Hearth's light** (a flame meter for the night, with the real elapsed time small beneath it — the word "midnight" is reserved for the Finale's countdown), the hint bell ("Ask the Fire"), the flowchart after every chapter, save/resume.

**The Companion (phones):** each player picks a Sighting and types their **real first name on their own phone** (private; the Hearth never asks for it — it is used once, at the end). Three tabs: **SIGHT** (gift pages for the current chapter plus persistent pages), **WREN** (private story: memories, whispers, letters), **SPEAK** (tokens and timed mini-tasks). Every SIGHT page shows "page x of n" for the chapter so people know when they are done reading. House rule, shown on both screens, never enforced by code: *"Say what you see. Never show your phone."* In-fiction: a Sighting is yours; another's eyes cannot read it.

**Setup, one screen each, before any story:** (1) seats and phones — fixed seating left to right: Reader, Listener, Seer, Binder; each player picks a role and types a first name on the phone; (2) key claim — each seat's lantern glows in turn; press your key (defaults `KeyA`, `KeyC`, `KeyM`, `Slash` by physical `event.code`; remappable; modifiers are never accepted as lanes); (3) **hold test** — "all four hold for three seconds": four flames on screen, any that gutter reveal ghosting; on failure the Hearth offers remaps, a second USB keyboard (both fire keydown), a **gamepad** (Gamepad API, two players on a pad, two on keys), or **Two-Hands mode** (every four-key chord becomes two pairs within 300 ms, pairs within 1 s; the Bells run as two shared lanes with a flowchart note); (4) a 20-s practice round ("when your lantern glows, press") ending in one Four Hands chord. Input rules for implementers: use `event.code`, drop `event.repeat`, ignore keys while any text input is focused, `preventDefault` on the four bound codes, never bind modifiers.

**Attunement words and the cast:** at each chapter boundary the Hearth shows one word carved into the scene, followed by a two-character **cast** ("the word, and the mark beside it"): `KNOT·7F`. Everyone types word and cast into their phones to unlock the chapter's pages. The cast is 10 bits in a 32-symbol alphabet (`ABCDEFGHJKLMNPQRSTUVWXYZ23456789`): up to 6 flag bits that *this chapter's* phone pages consume plus a 4-bit checksum, so every flag-dependent phone page in this document is buildable (register in Appendix B). The words are **KINDLE, THORN, KNOT, VEIL, EMBER, ASH, WELL, CROWN, WREN**. (Secret: after KINDLE they are the seven written glyphs of the Great Sigil in wall order; the eighth, COLD, is "never written" — until it is, at the climax, when WREN is typed. See §5, Finale, and §8.) One extra mini-word, **LINEN**, gates the Chapter 3 whispers (§5).

**Chapter boundaries are a designed beat ("the Sighting," 90 s, visible timer):** the flowchart auto-advances → the word and cast appear → "Read your Sight. Say nothing." → the Hearth names the chapter's **Warden of the Hearth** (keyboard) and **Voice** (reads the Hearth aloud) so neither piles on one person. Each chapter's minute budget in §10 includes its Sighting.

**Tokens:** phones talk back only through 3-letter tokens from a 23-letter alphabet (no O, I, S): letters 1–2 encode role, beat and choice from per-role tables; letter 3 is a checksum. The phone shows exactly one token, only after the choice is made, and never the choice→token mapping; the Hearth answers only "Received" or "The fire does not know that word," rejects a token from the wrong beat, and lets a player re-type until the beat closes (last one wins), so a mistype is never fatal. Twelve tokens all game (Ch3 ×4, Ch5 ×4, Finale ×4). Every private beat exists on all four phones, and the Hearth calls all four to SPEAK at the same moment, so nobody can infer whose page mattered.

**Timed mini-task:** Chapter 5's Founders' Count: the Hearth counts 3-2-1, all four press Start, 45 s each; each phone yields one digit; the four digits are typed into the Hearth as one number.

**Pass the controller and the Voice:** Prologue — Warden anyone, Voice Reader. Ch1 — Binder / Listener. Ch2 — Seer / Reader. Ch3 — Listener / Binder. Ch4 — pass-around / Seer. Ch5 — Reader / Listener. Ch6 — all four keys / Listener. Finale — pass-around by name, then keys / Binder. The driver can never solve a lock alone: every lock needs WHAT (Reader), WHEN (Listener), WHERE (Seer), WHETHER (Binder).

**Four-key mode ("Four Hands"):** every sigil closes with all four keys tapped within 1 s — sequential keydowns, safe on any keyboard — a ritual that drills the Bells (Ch6) and the Binding (Finale). **No mechanic in the game requires four keys to be held at once** (the Binding is a toggle, §7).

**Hints:** every lock has a 3-tier ladder: tier 1 names *which phone* holds the missing piece, tier 2 states the Law or reading, tier 3 gives — and where the lock is a ring, *places* — the solution. Tier 1 is auto-offered at the lock's par time (§10). Cost is cosmetic ("wax") plus a flowchart note. Ladders also exist for the Second Asking, the Stone and the Binding's release (§10). A separate accessibility menu (text for all audio, slow Bells, larger windows) costs nothing.

**Save/resume:** state is written to localStorage on every state transition (one `JSON.stringify`): chapter, flags, tokens received, hidden counters, hint tiers, elapsed time. The Hearth's pause screen shows a copyable base64 resume string (any length) and, as a typed fallback for another laptop, a 12-character code (chapter + flags + checksum) that resets elapsed time and hint counts with a flowchart note ("the fire was relit"). Phones store role, first name, unlocked chapters and their own token choices; a dead phone rejoins by choosing its role and typing the current word and cast.

**Components (four interactive, two presentational).** Every branch in this document is text substitution over these: **Ring** (all sigils, including a 3-glyph door and a linear "Strip" layout for the Stone; shows a sunwise arrow and the Seer-called marks), **Grid** (Ch3), **Lanes** (Bells and Binding), **Card** (every choice, timed or not; the Ch1 vote is a Card with nine seats). Presentational: **Map**, **Flowchart**.

**Arcs and private dilemmas:**
- **Reader:** knows what words mean and cannot read their friend's name — it is written on the dormitory door in the older alphabet, the one they have not learned. Ch3: Wren asks what *Wrenn* means (TELL — a bluff, "a small brave bird" / I DON'T KNOW YET — the truth). Ch4: while ciphering the Provost's journal, the Reader decodes the name themselves. True ending: Wren's goodbye is a line of glyphs they will not be able to read tomorrow.
- **Listener:** hears every heart but Wren's and has called it a fault in the gift. Ch3: "Can you hear mine?" (YES, LOUD — a lie / NO — the truth). Ch6: the voice of the table in the blind round. True ending: a heartbeat, finally, then the phone dies.
- **Seer:** has been dismissing the one thing that matters. Ch3: "What do you see when you look at me?" (TELL — the shadow / SAY NOTHING). Ch4: scrapes the tapestry and finds Vane told the truth. Ch6: sees the foot of the stone. True ending: a drawing of four shadows falling the right way.
- **Binder:** ties oaths and holds a struck Law. Ch3: "Do you think I'm really the one?" (YES / I DON'T KNOW). Ch4: chooses the oath's lock, KNOT or EMBER, which changes the Finale's geometry. Ch5–6: reads the 212 page and sees the cover-up before anyone says it. True ending: a thread finally drawn between two names.

## 4. World and backstory

**What players are told.** The Marches of Cael: a cold border realm of nine Houses, ruled from the distant capital by a Crown that resents the one place it does not own — **Thornhallow**, a fortress-school on top of the **Cold**, a wound through which the world's warmth leaks. Four hundred years ago four Founders — **Halvard the Gate, Idony the Binder, Rook the Veil, Mere the Keeper** — closed it and left behind the **Hearth**, a fire that has never gone out and whose light holds the seal. Their order, the Vigil, sorts Wardens by Sighting (Glyph, Ear, Eye, Thread); its politics are the **Convocation** of nine Masters, one per House, chaired by **Provost Ilsabet Marrow**, whose seal and mark is the glyph CROWN.

Fourteen years ago the Hearth guttered for one night. When it came back a baby lay on the warm stones. Marrow named it **Wren**, from the old tongue. The prophecy stone over the Hearth is read, by the Order's translation: *"When the Hearth goes cold, one born of four shall walk into the Cold, and it shall close behind them."* The Order decided "born of four" meant the four Founders' bloodlines and that the child was Wren. Wren has been raised knowing it and is somehow cheerful: a kind, funny fourteen-year-old who follows the fourth-years around, gives everyone nicknames, and once, by accident, called the Provost "Mum" in front of them.

Tonight is the Vigil, when Wren is formally named. **Lord Cassian Vane**, the Crown's Envoy, has arrived with soldiers and a writ to take Wren "for safekeeping"; the Crown wants the Cold *open*, because what leaks from it can be harnessed. And tonight, for the first time in fourteen years, the Hearth is flickering.

**What is true — in three layers, revealed in order (§8).**
- **(A) Wren is not human.** Wren is not a vessel *for* the leak; Wren *is* the leak, given flesh — the piece of the Cold that came out the night the Hearth guttered. Cold seeks warmth: that is why Wren's shadow falls toward every fire, why there is no heartbeat, why the Binder finds no thread ("the knot itself").
- **(B) The Founders were four, and there is another way.** They did not send one. Four who loved the world walked in together, gave up their Sightings, and *wrote* the glyph COLD with four hands — a seal made of warmth given freely. They came out grey-eyed and ordinary; the Hearth is only the fire they left behind. The prophecy stone says so, if read the way it was carved: **turned**. The Order has read it upright for four hundred years, because its foot — where the mark is — has been hidden by the flame since the night it was lit.
- **(C) Marrow's choice, and the Order's.** Either road needs the thing that is sealed to be loved: the Founders' seal needs four Sighted who love what they close; the Order's road needs the leak itself to walk back warm enough to close the wound behind it — a thing raised unloved would go back as cold as it came. In 212 the Convocation, facing a failing seal, struck Law 0 and wrote Law 6 in the same year, because "four hands" meant four Masters losing their Sightings — their power — and sent one Warden instead. Marrow, fourteen years ago, chose the road that costs one and told herself the one was not a person. *"Both roads needed him loved. One road needed him alone."*

**Vane** is not a stock villain. Twenty-two years ago he was an Eye-Sighted Warden here, expelled for scraping paint off the Hall tapestry and seeing four figures walk into the flame. He is the only adult who ever tried to stop a child being burned, nobody believed him *because he became the Envoy*, and he serves a Crown he has told himself will keep the boy alive. He is the source of revelation (B) all night; the game makes it easy to distrust him. The Binder sees him pulled by two threads, red (an oath to Wren) and gold.

**How the world unfolds:** dormitory → the Great Hall and the nine Houses → the Founders' Vault and the sealed road beneath → the Gallery's hidden corridors → the Provost's study → the Long Stair down past the school's foundations into the **Under-Marches**, a cavern with the drowned First Hall and four thrones, the Cold glowing below like an inverted sky → the bell-chamber whose floor is a lid → in the epilogue, the Marches and the capital beyond.

## 5. Chapter by chapter

Sigil conventions: **ring slots** are numbered sunwise (clockwise; an arrow is drawn on the Hearth ring and the Seer's ring page); the Seer's **mark** is the starting slot. Inscriptions have a mark at one end: mark on the **left** = upright, read left to right; mark on the **right** = **turned**: read right to left and every glyph inverts (ASH↔COLD, THORN↔WELL, KNOT↔VEIL, CROWN↔EMBER). A glyph never changes its place on the stone — only its reading. Every sigil closes with Four Hands. A wrong sigil: the ring frosts, resets, and a low the Listener-pulse plays; three wrongs auto-offer the next hint tier.

### Prologue — The Night the Hearth Guttered (10 min)
**Beats.** Setup screens (§2). Cold open: four hundred years of fire in one procedural animation, one night of dark, a baby on the stones. The prophecy stone in the Order's translation, the flame licking its foot. Cut to the dormitory, the night before the Vigil. The Hearth warns: *"Later tonight the bells of Thornhallow will ring. You will need these keys and each other. We will tell you before it happens."* Wren bursts in, hands out nicknames, says "Mum'll — the Provost'll — kill me if she finds the lamp lit," and dares them to light the forbidden lamp "the old way. Carve my name in it first — go on." The driver types **WREN** into the lamp; it flickers and dies. "Told you. Now do it properly." Attunement: KINDLE (no cast).

**Tutorial sigil — the Dormitory Lamp** (4-slot ring; carving above it of two shapes).
- Reader: carving = Flame, Crown-inverted → ASH, EMBER ("fire, keep").
- Seer: the carving's mark is on the left (upright); the ring's scratch is at slot 3.
- Listener: two notes, "up three" — the set {ASH, EMBER} in that contour is ASH then EMBER only.
- Binder Law 1 (Founders'): a sigil is read sunwise from the mark.
- **Solution:** ASH@3, EMBER@4, then Four Hands. Hints: (1) "The Seer knows where a ring begins." (2) "Sunwise from slot 3." (3) places it.

**Seeds (private WREN tab, no token; anomalies only, never a sentence):** Seer — the under-layer of the lit dormitory: four shadows away from the lamp, one toward it; Listener — the heartbeat page: five names, one flat, "too quiet to catch"; Reader — "Wren's name on the door is in the older alphabet. Everyone else's is in the Vigil's script. You cannot read it yet"; Binder — "the practice thread that would not hold: not unbound; the knot itself."

**Choice:** flavour only — what Wren calls the group ("the Four" / "the Idiots" / "the Vigil-in-waiting"). First flowchart: one lit node, six grey branches fanning out.

### Chapter 1 — The Vigil (13 min) — Binder drives, Listener is the Voice
**Beats.** The Great Hall; the nine Houses' banners; Wren presented. Vane enters with the writ and one line for the Provost: *"I have seen what is under the paint in this hall, Ilsabet."* Marrow forces a vote of the nine Masters on whether Wren stays tonight and tells the four: "You have until the bell to talk to whoever you must." The Hearth flickers; Marrow looks at Wren, not the fire. Attunement: THORN (no cast).

**The Convocation Vote** (deduction; a six-minute bell, narrative — at 6:00 Marrow "stalls the Convocation" and the ladder's last tier fires; tier 1 auto at 3:00, tier 2 at 4:30). Nine seats sunwise from the Chair (seat 9), shown on the Hearth by House banner only. Rule card on the Hearth: *You may approach two Masters before the bell. An approached Master votes KEEP — unless they hold Crown coin (they vote SEND whoever asks) or cannot be approached. A Master with a thread follows it unless approached. An undecided Master votes SEND unless approached. Five of nine keeps Wren.*
- Reader: the decoded seat list (no cipher work): 1 Sorrel (Thread), 2 Quill (Glyph), 3 Brack (Eye), 4 Hallan (Ear), 5 Vey (Glyph), 6 Orrin (Thread), 7 Oriel (Eye), 8 Tarn (Ear), 9 **Marrow — the Chair; the Chair's mark: CROWN** (KEEP, cannot be approached).
- Seer: Crown coin under seats 5 and 8; Vane's guard behind seat 6 (Orrin cannot be approached).
- Listener murmurs: seat 1 "The boy goes to the capital unless someone asks me to my face"; seat 3 "I stand with the Provost"; seat 4 "I vote as Orrin votes and hear no one else" (cannot be approached).
- Binder threads: Quill follows Sorrel; Orrin votes opposite Brack; Oriel "undecided — a thread waiting to be tied."
- Working: base KEEP = Marrow, Brack (2); Orrin opposite Brack → SEND, Hallan → SEND; Sorrel, Quill, Oriel, Vey, Tarn → SEND. Two approaches must add three: only Sorrel adds two (Quill follows); the last must come from a persuadable non-follower — Oriel. **Unique solution: approach Sorrel and Oriel** (verified over every pair). Vote 5–4 KEEP. **On failure** (VOTE_LOST): Vane takes Wren to the Vigil dais under guard; Marrow: "Then bring the Ember. I will get the child back myself." Ch2 plays identically; WREN_TRUST −1; Ch3's opening paragraph changes. Hints: (1) "Two seats are bought — The Seer can see the coin." (2) "Who follows whom? The Binder's threads; the Listener's murmurs say who will listen." (3) solution.

**Prices (timed group choice, 45 s):** the two persuaded Masters name prices; honour one, or neither.
- SORREL: "The Cold Ember, when you bring it up, comes to the Convocation, not to the Provost." → SORREL: the Ember is handed over at the end of Ch2 (EMBER_LOST — one of the Silent Gate's bells is cracked, §5 Ch5); Sorrel's writ gives a clean fourth option at the Ch3 tower door; in the Finale Sorrel holds the Convocation's guards at the stair. Refuse her and she comes down with them.
- ORIEL: "Tell me what you find below. All of it." → ORIEL: in Ch4 her note arrives (un-strikes Law 0); she stands with the four in the Finale.
- Neither → Marrow's trust rises: in Ch4 her unsent letter appears (a clue) — but both Masters come down with Tarn at the end.

**Vane's offer (timed, 60 s):** alone with the four: "Bring the boy to me before dawn. He lives — I promise you that — and the Crown makes you Masters. You think I am the villain of this night. Ask your Seer what is under the paint." REFUSE / PRETEND TO ACCEPT / ACCEPT. Pretend unlocks BLUFF in Ch3. Accept: the Ch3 captain lets Wren pass on Vane's word; Marrow's Ch4 letter never appears; Vane's Finale offer is warmer. Flowchart: vote node, prices node, Vane node.

**Foreshadowing:** Vane's paint line, twice; Marrow looking at Wren; the heartbeat page lists nine Masters, Vane (fast), Wren (flat).

### Chapter 2 — The Ember Vault (14 min) — Seer drives, Reader is the Voice
**Beats.** Marrow sends the four beneath the school for the Cold Ember, "which relights the Hearth if it gutters again." Myst-style point-and-click through the Stair, the Founders' Antechamber (four statues, four dials), the Vault. The Map unfolds downward and shows the Founders' road continuing past the vault, bricked up. Attunement: KNOT·cast (VOTE_LOST).

**The Founders' Door.** Four statues, left to right **Mere, Halvard, Rook, Idony** (the vault was rebuilt in 212 and the statues re-set), each apparently facing the dial in front of it: Mere→A, Halvard→B, Rook→C, Idony→D.
- Reader + Seer: epitaph shapes — Mere *Crown* (mark **right**, plinth re-set upside down) = EMBER; Halvard *Spike* (mark left) = THORN; Rook *Hook* (mark **right**) = VEIL; Idony *Hook* (mark left) = KNOT. Read naively: CROWN, THORN, KNOT, KNOT — the door refuses.
- Seer: the under-layer shows the original plinth sockets; the true sightlines are Halvard→C, Idony→A, Rook→D, Mere→B.
- Listener: the Founders' phrase, "up one, up three, down two." Over the set {THORN, KNOT, VEIL, EMBER} the only fit is THORN, KNOT, VEIL, EMBER (verified).
- Binder Law 2 (Founders'): *each Founder's glyph sits on the dial they face; turn the dials in the Hymn's order; the door counts your turns and forgets a wrong count.* Law 9 (Order's, 212): *a Founder faces the dial before them.* Law 3 (Founders'): *where two Laws disagree, the older binds* — so the sockets, not the rebuild.
- **Solution:** C=THORN, then A=KNOT, then D=VEIL, then B=EMBER, in that order. Hints: (1) "Two plinths are upside down — The Seer knows which; the Reader's lexicon has both readings." (2) "The statues lie about where they look — the older Law binds; the Listener has the order." (3) solution.

**Mere's Niche (optional, missable).** The Seer's under-layer shows a niche behind Mere's plinth; the Hearth never hints unless asked (one tier: "The Seer — behind Mere"). Inside: a stone strip of three glyphs and a sheet in the older alphabet. The strip, shapes left to right: Spike-inverted, Crown-inverted, Hook-inverted. A Reader working alone reads it upright — WELL, EMBER, VEIL: *"went down · kept · alone"* — the Order's version, "one went down alone and kept it." The Seer's under-layer puts the mark on the **right**: read right to left, inverted — KNOT, CROWN, THORN: *"four · as one · went through."* Nobody at the table can yet say four what, or through what. The sheet cannot be read; the Reader "takes a rubbing" (click) → LETTER: the rubbing sits on the Reader's phone, illegible, until Chapter 4. Flowchart shows "Found Mere's niche?" grey if missed — a deliberate itch.

**Timed choice (30 s)** as the stair falls: Wren has followed ("Wren has never been where Wren was put") and stands across the gap with the Ember case. GRAB WREN (the case falls: EMBER_LOST) / GRAB THE EMBER (WREN_HURT — in Ch3 the Laundry doors each cost a turn, and every whisper changes). If SORREL, the Ember goes to Sorrel at the top (EMBER_LOST).

**Foreshadowing:** the strip; the under-layer draws Wren's shadow reaching toward the glowing case; four statues, four thrones on the Map below; the bricked road.

### Chapter 3 — The Whispering Gallery (14 min) — Listener drives, Binder is the Voice

## 7. The everyone-together climax — The Great Sigil, the Binding, and writing COLD

Pressing again early drops your note and the flame dips ("Four hands. Hold."). After ~6 s the Hymn reaches its rest — and the Hearth gives **no count**: the four must release (press again) together, within 500 ms of each other, counting themselves in aloud. Feedback is exact ("The Seer released at +0.31 s"). Each failure burns 30 s of midnight; after three failures the Hearth counts the release for them (stat "with help"). Cracked bells remove that lane's cue; the Listener's phone has the Binding count ("press on 'four'; release on 'one-two-three-off'") and calls it. A kept bargain leaves that key dead: three-handed Binding, hold 8 s, one note missing.

**Writing COLD (true path).** After the Binding, if the Fourfold Walk was chosen and every token was WALK: the Hearth shows the COLD slot empty with Wren standing in it — *"It is never written."* — and then the last attunement: **WREN·cast**, *"Write it."* Everyone types it. Each phone in turn, in seat order, five seconds apart, shows Wren's goodbye **by that player's real first name** (glyphs the Reader will not be able to read tomorrow / a heartbeat / four shadows falling the right way / a thread between two names) and burns to black: *"Your Sighting is spent. Look up."* Nothing is left on the table but the keyboard and each other. Then the Hearth: *"Wren's hand closes over the hand on the key. 'Write it.'"* — **Four Hands.** The inverted flame appears; Wren steps aside; the four step through. White.

## 8. The twist, the clue trail, the endings

**The twist, in three revelations that need different evidence.** (A) Wren is not human — the leak given flesh; (B) the Founders were four and there is another way — carved on the stone the Order has read upside down for four hundred years; (C) both roads need Wren loved, Marrow chose the road that costs one, and the Order struck the Law that would have cost four Masters their power. Only (C) is told; (A) is confessed after Wren's permission; (B) the players produce themselves, with the grammar they have drilled all night, from the stone in the room.

**The fair-clue trail (every clue, where):**
1. Prologue — the prophecy in the Order's translation, the stone's foot in flame; the four shadows away, one toward; the flat line "too quiet to catch"; "not unbound; the knot itself"; a name the Reader cannot read; Law 0 struck, 212.
2. Ch1 — Vane: "I have seen what is under the paint," and "Ask your Seer"; Marrow looks at Wren when the fire flickers; the Chair's mark is CROWN.
3. Ch2 — the niche strip: "four · as one · went through" (only with two Sightings); the shadow reaching for the Ember; four statues, four thrones; the bricked road; the 212 rebuild.
4. Ch3 — Wren asks each player exactly the question their gift answers; the portraits: "four went down."
5. Ch4 — WRENN = a hollow, and COLD's gloss; the journal ("it laughs at my jokes"); the memory-bell ("through me. And through it"); the tapestry (four figures, the fourth hand writing COLD — Vane was right); the grey thread; Mere's rubbing; Oriel's note / Marrow's unsent letter (conditional).
6. Ch5 — Law 6 vs. the struck Law at the collapse; three Order's Laws dated 212 against the Founders'; the four thrones; the empty slot in the Silent Gate — or four hands on it.
7. Ch6 — Wren's replies; "I know, Mum"; the stone's one rest; the Founders' reading.
8. Bonus — the attunement words are the Great Sigil's order; the eighth was a dare.

**Endings and conditions** (one scene body with variable paragraphs; the scene names who walked and who stayed before the flowchart):
- **THE FOURFOLD WALK (true):** Walk unlocked and chosen, no bargain kept, Binding landed, all four tokens WALK. COLD written by four hands after the phones die; the Hearth roars white; the four come out grey-eyed and ordinary; Wren, **with a heartbeat now**, waits on the stones. Vane's report to the Crown says the Cold is closed and there is nothing to harness — the first true thing he has ever sent them. Epilogue, years later: four unremarkable people who **miss it** — they argue every winter about what the ring looked like and cannot agree; the Reader keeps a letter they cannot read; the Listener says the house is too quiet — and would do it again; Wren, grown, visiting, with a pulse the Listener cannot hear and does not need to.
- **THE HALF-WALK (bittersweet):** Walk chosen but only two or three WALK, or one bargain kept. The Cold closes narrower; Wren lives, **without a heartbeat** — the one visible difference; walkers come out free; stayers keep their Sightings and the fire for life, the new Masters of a school that knows.
- **THE SEALING (tragic-heroic):** LET WREN WALK. "It's alright. I knew." Four hundred years of fire; a fifth name carved over the Hearth; Marrow alone with the grey thread; the flowchart shows "They went in together" one step away.
- **THE KEEPER'S WALK:** REFUSE, or the Walk chosen with zero or one WALK — Marrow's own counter-offer: "Then I go. I should have gone fourteen years ago." She walks in; the seal holds thin; Wren lives without a heartbeat. Epilogue: Wren as Provost, keeping a fire that flickers.
- **THE ENVOY'S BARGAIN (bad):** GIVE WREN TO VANE, or two or more bargains kept in the room. Wren in a cage; the Cold feeds the Crown's engines; Thornhallow a garrison; the four, Masters of ash. Broken bargains show as "one of you almost did."
- **COLD HEARTH (fail):** midnight reaches zero. "Some nights simply end." Resume from CROWN; the first attempt is recorded.

**Phones at the end.** WREN·cast carries the ending. Walkers' phones burn with the goodbye by real name; a stayer's phone shows "You kept your Sight. You watched them go." In the Sealing, every phone shows Wren's last nickname for that player and goes dark; in the Keeper's Walk, Marrow's one line to each; in the Bargain, a Crown seal.

## 9. The core mechanic and its escalation

**The Fourfold Sigil.** Every lock is a ring of slots filled with glyphs from the Founders' Tongue: four shapes, each read two ways by orientation — Flame ASH/COLD, Spike THORN/WELL, Hook KNOT/VEIL, Crown CROWN/EMBER. No glyph can be read without the Seer's call (upright or turned), no word without the Reader, no order without the Listener's steps, and nothing can be trusted without the Binder's dated Laws — and none of the four can do another's job, because the Listener hears steps, not names, and the Reader reads shapes, not marks. Escalation by breaking its own rules in announced ways: Prologue two glyphs, one Law → Ch2 turned epitaphs, turned statues, and two Laws that disagree → Ch4 a whole turned line and a lock-glyph that is a choice → Ch5 a turned gate placed widdershins, a silent gate counted in pulses, a glyph that must not be written → Ch6 the prophecy itself, read the way it was carved → Finale eight glyphs, two walls, two marks, seven Laws, a rotation earned by an oath, and the forbidden glyph written by four hands. Around it, the same four gifts drive non-sigil forms every chapter (a vote, a stealth grid, a study search, a per-phone count, bells) so the grammar is the language, not the only sentence. The story's answer — COLD is written by four hands — is the rule the players have been enacting since the dormitory.

## 11. Known risks and mitigations

1. **Pacing density (Ch3, Ch5, Finale).** Ch4 trimmed to 12 and Ch5 given 17; the tower sigil is two glyphs; auto-hints at par; the Finale's tier 2 states widdershins, the empty slot and the rotation outright, and tier 3 places the ring rather than printing it.
2. **Phone audio.** Tap-to-play for iOS; arrow strips make every contour readable without ears; text fallbacks at no story cost.
3. **Keyboards.** Hold test in setup, remaps, second keyboard, gamepad, Two-Hands mode; `event.code`, no modifiers, no repeats, `preventDefault`; and no mechanic holds four keys — the Binding toggles.
4. **The house rule is unenforceable.** Restated before every token moment; tokens never show their mapping; the design survives broken secrecy (the reveal is Wren's identity, and Wren's replies are scripted from what the Hearth received).
5. **Twist guessable early.** Layered: anomalies only until Ch4, the "other way" from Ch4, Marrow's choice only in Ch6; a table that guesses (A) in the vault still has to earn (B) on the stone and produce the ring.
6. **Sealing is easiest.** The Walk unlocks from a lock with a ladder, not from a hidden count; the epilogue shows the grey "together" node and offers the Finale from CROWN; the Keeper's Walk catches tables that unlock the Walk and then flinch.
7. **Branch cost.** Variants reuse the four components with re-dressed text; one Finale scene with swapped paragraphs; every phone variation is driven by a cast bit (Appendix B); the vote-failure re-dress of Ch2 is cut to one flag and one paragraph.
8. **Token friction.** Twelve tokens, 3 letters, checksum, wrong-beat rejection, re-typing allowed, instant "Received."
9. **The spent volunteer's downtime.** Their phone becomes The Thread for two rounds only, with a live job, and their Sight returns for Round 3.
10. **A dominant player.** Warden and Voice named per chapter; the driver cannot solve alone; Round 3 hands the table to the Listener; Finale placement is by name only.
11. **Phone/Hearth sync.** Nothing scrolls on a phone against the Hearth's clock; Round 3 is a static list against a Hearth beat counter; the Count is a 45-s task started on a 3-2-1; the only phone→Hearth data is tokens.
12. **Accessibility.** Lanes labelled by nickname and glyph, not colour alone; slow bells; larger windows; text for all audio; "sunwise = clockwise" and an arrow on every ring.
13. **Tone.** The twist is dark; Wren's humour, the replies in the bell-chamber, the goodbyes by real name and an epilogue in which the four *miss it and would do it again* carry the warmth so the night ends on four friends, not a sacrifice.

## Appendix B — Flag register and cast bits (for implementers)

**Hearth-side state (saved on every transition):** chapter; VOTE_LOST; SORREL, ORIEL, NEITHER; VANE_PRETEND, VANE_ACCEPT; LETTER (rubbing taken), LETTER_READ; EMBER_LOST; WREN_HURT; WREN_SCARED; whisper tokens ×4 (truth/lie per role); door outcome (FIGHT / BLUFF / WORD / WRIT / SURRENDERED); JOURNAL, MEMORY, TAPESTRY, GREY (found); OATH (none / KNOT / EMBER); LAW0 (= LETTER_READ ∨ TAPESTRY ∨ ORIEL); stair outcome (COLLAPSE / VOLUNTEER id / SOLDIERS); BELLS_CRACKED 0–3; CLUES 0–4; WALK_UNLOCKED; VANE_ALLY; decision; bargains (accepted / broken / kept per role); WALK tokens; WREN_TRUST; hint tiers used; elapsed time.

**Cast bits per attunement (6 data bits + 4-bit checksum, 32-symbol alphabet):**
- KINDLE, THORN: no cast.
- KNOT: b0 VOTE_LOST.
- VEIL: b0 VOTE_LOST, b1 WREN_HURT, b2 VANE_ACCEPT. (LINEN: no cast.)
- EMBER: b0 LETTER, b1 ORIEL, b2 SORREL, b3 VANE_ACCEPT, b4 SURRENDERED, b5 WREN_SCARED.
- ASH: b0–1 OATH (00 none, 01 KNOT, 10 EMBER), b2 EMBER_LOST, b3 LAW0, b4 VANE_ACCEPT, b5 WREN_HURT.
- WELL: b0–2 VOLUNTEER (0 none, 1 Reader, 2 Listener, 3 Seer, 4 Binder), b3 PRECRACKED.
- CROWN: b0 WALK_UNLOCKED, b1 VANE_ALLY, b2–3 BELLS_CRACKED, b4 OATH_KNOT.
- WREN: b0–2 ENDING (0 Fourfold, 1 Half, 2 Sealing, 3 Keeper's, 4 Bargain). Each phone combines this with its own stored WALK/STAY token and first name to render its last page.

**Tokens (12):** Ch3 whispers ×4 (2 choices each); Ch5 hold ×4 (YES/NO; first YES typed wins); Finale ×4 (WALK/STAY × ACCEPT/REFUSE, or the subset that exists). Alphabet `ABCDEFGHJKLMNPQRTUVWXYZ`; letter 3 = (index₁ + 2·index₂) mod 23.

## Appendix C — Implementation notes (post-build)

- The cast is shown at each chapter's attunement, so the finale phones' question set is fixed before Stage 1; the Hearth voids a bargain answer if Vane stands down afterwards (see docs/CONVENTIONS.md §12).
- Casts are three symbols (6 data bits + 9 check bits), not two: a mistyped mark is rejected 99.8% of the time instead of 94%.
- Tokens are four symbols from a 32-symbol alphabet, derived per (beat, role, value) with collision-free salting; the Hearth decodes by comparison, so a wrong-beat token is simply "not attuned".
- Ring and dial palettes show glyph names only; the shape→word lexicon exists only on the Reader's phone.
