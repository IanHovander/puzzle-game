## 0. SCOPE

### 0.1 The mystery, stated exactly

Three questions the game raises about Provost Ilsabet Marrow and answers at three different depths:

| # | the question | how the game answers it | where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M1** | **What does she know?** | Fully, but scattered across nine chapters, four phones and two optional branches. She knows more than any other living character, including Vane. | §0.2 inventory |
| **M2** | **When did she decide?** | Once, fourteen years ago, at the moment of naming. She has never revisited it. The game says so four separate times and never in one place. | `ch7.js:367`; `ch8.js:333`; `ch6.js:692`; `ch7.js:777` |
| **M3** | **What is she protecting?** | **Six things at once, and they are in conflict.** The game never enumerates them; it dramatises one per chapter. | §0.4 |

**The short answer the tables are built on.** Marrow knows the school's reading of the prophecy is
a forgery and has known since she was a girl with a bread-knife (`ch7.js:394`). She knows what 212
did and why (`ch7.js:396`). She knows what is under the school (`ch5.js:249`). She knows the fire is
four spent people and is therefore unrepairable (`ch6.js:850`). She can read the true stone herself
(`ch6.js:824`). **And with all of that she built, fourteen years ago, a plan around one walker** —
because the true reading's price is four Sightings and she does not have four volunteers, and the one
body she does have is a child she found on the stones and named *hollow*. She then spent fourteen
years making that child loved enough to go (`ch6.js:692`). Her secret is not a fact. **Her secret is
that she ran the 212 calculation again and got the same answer, with better motives** — and that she
knows it, which is what "I should have gone fourteen years ago" (`ch7.js:777`) means.

### 0.3 When she decided — the four statements, assembled

The game never puts these in one place. Assembled, they are a single dated decision.

| statement | cite | what it fixes |
|---|---|---|
| "I picked it up. **I named it.** I raised it to be— / '*Loved*,' says Wren. '**Loved enough to walk back in**'" | `ch6.js:691-692` | The purpose was **contemporaneous with the naming**. Not a plan she arrived at; a plan she began with. |
| "**The ring has been ready for fourteen years.**" | `ch7.js:367` | Physical preparation began the same year. "Ready" implies work (§12.22). |
| Her thread to Wren is grey — "a goodbye **already said**" — and has been "**for fourteen years**" | `companion/ch6.js:268`; `ch8.js:333` | The grief is not anticipatory. She said goodbye at the start. |
| "Then I go. **I should have gone fourteen years ago.**" | `ch7.js:777` | The alternative she weighed and rejected fourteen years ago was **walking in herself**. |

**Ruling for the tables: T-14 years is a single night on which she found a child, identified it,
named it for what it was, considered going in herself, declined, and began preparing the ring.**
Everything from T0 to T9 is the execution of a decision taken before the game's fourteen-year prologue
began. She does not decide anything during the game. She only yields, once, at T9.

**[PROPOSED]** The naming is the decision, and the game can say so in one clause. WRENN means *the
hollow of a bell — the space inside it that makes the sound* (`companion/ch4.js:202-204`). To name the
child *the hollow* is to have already classified it as the thing that goes back. Nobody in the game
remarks that she chose that word, in a dead alphabet, in her own hand, on an official roll.

## 12. THE GAP THAT DRIVES THE DRAMA

### 12.1 The pair: **Marrow ↔ the Binder**

Not Marrow ↔ Wren (they agree, and have since the laundry), and not Marrow ↔ Vane (he is right and
she knows it, so there is no tension, only a lever). The productive asymmetry is:

| | Marrow | the Binder |
|---|---|---|
| holds | the plan, the history, the stone, the ring — and **a Book without Law 0** `ch5.js:399` | **the Book with Law 0**, text intact, from the Prologue `lore.js:57` (`learned:'ch0'`); `companion/book.js:123-127` |
| holds about the other | that the Binder is a fourth-year who keeps a rule-book and knows what wax takes `ch4.js:686` | that she is an authority, and — privately — a woman whose thread they have "**decided long ago not to look**" at `companion/ch3.js:276` |
| the asymmetry | She does not know the child in front of her is carrying the document that refutes her life's work | They do not know the document in their pocket refutes her life's work |
| what breaks it | **Nothing, ever.** No scene puts the question to either of them |

**Why this pair and not another.** It is the only pairing in the mystery where both parties are
*right about their own facts and wrong about each other's*, where the mismatch is **mechanically
enforced by the partition rule** (`lore.js:74`, "Say what you see. Never show your phone."), and where
closing it would change the ending. It also pays off both characters' arcs at once: her Epilogue
confession is "**I should have asked anyone**" `companion/ch8.js:197`, and the Binder's private
refusal is "**you decided long ago not to look**". **They are the same failure, seen from two seats,
and the game never lets them meet.**

## 14. WHERE BEHAVIOUR DOES NOT MATCH THE KNOWLEDGE STATE

Ruthless pass. Ordered by cost.

### (a) T3: she asserts the Order's reading from the dais, holding three independent refutations — **highest cost**

> `ch1.js:124` — "**The stone over your heads says one born of four.** Tonight I stop arguing and show you."

At T3 she already holds **K6** (she scraped the paint as a girl and saw four figures and no child,
`ch7.js:394`), **K7** (212 was a cost dodge dressed as grammar, `ch7.js:396`) and **K9** (she can read
the stone from its foot, `ch6.js:824`). She stands under the stone and quotes the forgery to the nine.

Three readings, and the game supports none of them explicitly:
1. **She is lying to the Convocation** to win a custody vote. Consistent with everything, and would
   make her the most interesting character in the game — but nothing marks it as a lie, and the
   narration is otherwise scrupulous about marking hers.
2. **She is quoting, not endorsing** — "the stone **says**" is a report of the school's text, and
   "tonight I stop arguing and show you" means *I stop arguing about the translation*. **This reading
   is available in the words and is killed by §12.42: nothing is shown.**
3. She does not yet hold K6/K7/K9 at T3 — impossible; K6 dates to her girlhood by her own account.

**[PROPOSED], one clause:** give reading 2 its payoff. Her next line after `ch1.js:124` becomes
"— and the stone is wrong. I will show you why, when the vote is done." The chapter then *has* a
promise it keeps at T8, and her T9 confession stops being a retcon.

### (b) T4: she sends four children for an object she never uses, with a reason her own T8 line contradicts

without it. Come up. All of you.**" `ch2.js:396` — no second attempt, no alarm, no mention of it in
three subsequent chapters including the one where the fire goes out. **Either the Ember does not
matter and she knows it, or she is uncharacteristically negligent about the school's only insurance
policy.** The first is supported by her own second sentence in the same speech —
"**take the Seer's eyes with you. That vault was rebuilt once, and the rebuilding was not honest**"
`ch2.js:189` — which is an instruction to *look*, not to *fetch*. **[PROPOSED]:** make the errand
openly an examination and the inconsistency evaporates.

### (c) T6: the most procedural person in the game leaves four investigators alone in her private study for ten minutes

> `ch4.js:355` — "The scroll is behind the third shelf. Read it. Argue. **I will be ten minutes.**"

"**back early, and does not say why**" (`ch4.js:607`, §12.44) and, seeing her wall scraped, says
"**So. The Seer.**" (`ch4.js:609`) — recognition, not anger.

A woman who files her own vote in writing before the doors shut (`companion/ch1.js:94`), doses every
lamp on a corridor personally (`ch3.js:445`) and brings chalk, salt and a seal to a lid (`ch6.js:484`)
does not leave that room by accident. **[PROPOSED], and it resolves §12.44 in one clause: the study is
an examination she set.** She cannot say *four* — her oath is written for one, her Chair forbids it,
and saying it makes her responsible for the four children who would then pay. So she leaves the
evidence out and goes away for ten minutes. If accepted, every withholding in this document becomes a
single consistent policy, and her Epilogue line "**I should have asked you sooner**"
(`companion/ch8.js:197`) becomes the admission that a prepared room is not the same as a question.

### (d) T7→T8: she learns the Binder's Book outranks hers and does not ask one question

> `ch5.js:399` — "That is not in the Book I was given."

She is four hundred feet above a ring she has kept ready for fourteen years, standing beside a
fourteen-year-old who has just produced a Law that licenses writing COLD, and **she does not ask what
it says.** She never asks again. Two chapters later she bars the Walk on legal grounds (`ch7.js:387`)
while the legal ground that defeats her is in the pocket of the child she is arguing with.

**This is the document's central behavioural finding.** It is not fixable by a flag; it needs a line.
**[PROPOSED]:** she asks, the Binder tells her, and she says something that is not an answer —
"Then it is a Law nobody has kept." That preserves the plot (she proceeds anyway), converts the gap
from oversight into refusal, and makes T9's yielding a completion rather than a reversal.

### (e) T8: she withholds a reading that costs her nothing to give and charges four children for it

> `ch6.js:823` — "**Four readings. Stop.** Hands off it — I have had four hundred years of this stone and you have had five minutes." / `:824` — "She kneels at the foot, puts one thumb in the first burn, and reads it the way the Founders cut it."

Mechanically, `WALK_UNLOCKED` and `LAW0` are set **regardless** of whether she read it or they did
(`ch6.js:814`). Her withholding therefore purchases nothing except `STONE_TOLD`, which costs the four
two minutes of the Finale (Appendix A). Her stated reason is a boast. **The in-fiction reason that
would work is never given:** a reading she hands them is a reading she has authored — which is exactly
what the Order did — and she needs the four to own it. **[PROPOSED]:** replace the boast with that,
in six words.

### (f) The whole game: she never once responds to the four anomalies — **[WITHHOLDING]**

She named the child *the hollow of a bell* (`companion/ch4.js:202-204`). She raised it fourteen years.
It has no heartbeat, no thread, and a shadow that leans into every fire. **She never refers to any of
it, to anyone, on any branch, including in the confession**, where she says only "It came out of the
fire" (`ch6.js:691`). The four say all four anomalies aloud at T2 and at T8, in her hearing at T8, and
she does not react to a single one.

Either she has spent fourteen years not looking — which is a devastating characterisation and would
need one line to establish — or this is a hole. **[PROPOSED], one line at `ch6.js:692`:** after
"Loved enough to walk back in", she adds *"I know what it has instead of a heartbeat. I named it for
it."* That single sentence closes §12.53, §12.67 and this finding together.

### (g) T5: "I have known since the laundry" refers to a scene the game does not contain — **[UNEARNED]**

`ch6.js:689` against `ch3.js:500`. See §6 above for the proposed Bess fix. The cost of leaving it: the
game's most important change in Wren's knowledge state about Marrow happens in a room the player
walked through without being told anything happened.

### (h) T5→T6: she judges the four for saving a life that was never in danger, and does not tell them

> `ch3.js:543` — the captain: "Hand over the boy, or **the Provost hangs.** The Envoy has her in the Great Hall with a rope over the beam."
> `ch3.js:635`, `:644-645` — on **every** branch: "**The rope came off the beam an hour ago.**"
> `ch4.js:340` — on `SURRENDERED`, her first words: "**You gave the child to a man with a writ.** Not now. Sit down."

She was at the bell-rope minutes before (`ch3.js:445`) and is in her study minutes after. Whether she
knows the threat was made is **never stated on any branch**. If she does, she is condemning four
children for a choice made to save her, while knowing the choice was based on a lie — **and saying
nothing about the lie.** That is either her worst moment or an oversight, and the game does not
distinguish. §12.39. **[PROPOSED]:** one clause — "There was never a rope. He knew that. **You did
not.** Sit down." — converts the beat into the chapter's best line about her.

### (i) T9: she and Vane scraped the same paint and neither acknowledges the other

> `ch7.js:341` — Vane: "Twenty-two years. I stood in your Hall with **that paint under my nails** and told them."
> `ch7.js:394` — Marrow, three scenes later, same room: "**I scraped it myself, as a girl.**"

Both lines can occur in one playthrough (`ch7_wall` → `ch7_attune` → `ch7_decision` → `ch7_argue1`).
**Two people who independently committed the same act of heresy against the same institution, in the
same building, decades apart, stand ten feet apart and do not look at each other.** §12.23. A single
reaction line is free and is the cheapest large improvement available to this mystery.

### (j) T9: she bars a door on law and opens it for a non-reason

`ch7.js:387` against `ch7.js:398` ("That is not a reading." … "**She steps aside anyway.**"). Her
best moment, and it is unmotivated as written. **[PROPOSED]:** it is motivated, and her Epilogue says
so — "I should have asked anyone" `companion/ch8.js:197`. One clause before she steps aside —
*"No. It is not."* — would let the player hear her accept a reason she cannot justify, which is the
whole character.

### (k) T10: she is absent from three of five endings, including the one that supersedes her

E0, E1 and E4 do not mention her (§12.30). **On ENDING 0 the four pay exactly the price she spent
fourteen years arranging for somebody else to pay, and the woman who arranged it has no reaction,
no line and no presence.** The Epilogue's closing image is five shapes at a window; she is not one of
them, and nothing says why. This is the largest structural hole in the mystery.

### (l) Throughout: she assigns work by gift and never once asks what the gifts have seen

She says "take the **Seer's** eyes with you" (`ch2.js:189`), "**Binder** — the lock is yours"
(`ch4.js:686`), "**Listener** — your bell goes quiet. You are the voice" (`ch6.js:595`), "one **Seer**
low enough to look" (`ch7.js:392`). She has a precise working model of all four Sightings. **She never
asks any of them a question about Wren.** Four people who have each privately carried an impossibility
about her child for years stand in front of her for eight chapters and she never asks. Consistent with
(f); needs the same one-line fix.
