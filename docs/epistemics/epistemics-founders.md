# EPISTEMICS — **What the four Founders did, and what it cost them**

> ## ⚠ TOTAL SPOILERS
> Every reveal in the game, including the Finale's and all five endings. Author-facing working
> document. Companion to `CANON.md` (ground truth) — this file is **belief**, and never restates truth
> except to say who is wrong about it.

**Scope.** One mystery — the Year-0 act and its price — across the spine T0–T10, for every character
with a stake. Truth lives in `CANON.md` §5, §7.3, §8, §9.6. This file answers only: *who holds which
of these fifteen propositions, when, how confidently, what they believe the others hold, and what
they will not say.*

**Why this mystery is not the Hearth mystery.** `epistemics-hearth.md` asks *what the fire is*. This
file asks *what four people did on one day four hundred years ago, and what it took out of them*. The
two meet at F7 and then separate again: the Hearth question is answered on the shared screen at T8;
**the cost question is never answered on the shared screen before T10.** That asymmetry is the whole
document.

**Method note.** `REFUSED_OATH` is treated as **unsworn**, never as *refused* (`CANON.md` §13.16).
Branch rows carry their flag. Inference not in the source is marked **[PROPOSED]**.

**Where:** ch7–ch8. **What it requires:** that F3, F7, F8 and F12 — the truth about the school's own foundation, plus documentary proof that the school's governing body forged the record — are revealed to four children and then never reach the nine, in a game whose Chapter I turns on those nine having a vote. §12.30. **The mystery's political half has no third act.** Oriel's price is explicitly "Tell me what you find down there. **All of it.** … **Even the parts you don't like.**" (`ch1.js:256`) and the game never stages the telling (§12.58).

### §3.9 The Founders legislated against being overruled, and nobody at the table notices they were right

**Where:** `lore.js:60` (Law 3, Founders', Year 0: "Where two Laws disagree, the older binds") vs `lore.js:57`, `:62`, `:66` (three Order Laws of 212, every one of them outranked by an older Founders' Law). **What it requires:** that the Binder — who uses Law 3 to beat Law 9 at T4, Law 5 to beat Law 11 at T7, and Law 0 to beat Law 6 at T8 — uses the Founders' anti-override clause **three times** and never remarks that the Founders wrote it *in Year 0, before anyone had tried.* That is the single most characterising fact about the four Founders available in the game, it is derivable from two rows of a table, and no one says it.

### §3.10 The table performs the Founders' act in the Prologue and again at the climax, and the game never links them

**Where:** `ch0.js:213-216` (the lamp: ASH, EMBER, four hands, "*Fire, keep.*") and `ch7.js:740` (COLD, four hands). **Nothing at the climax refers back to the lamp.** The four's first four-handed write and their last are the same gesture four hundred years and eight chapters apart, and the Epilogue's word-card scene (`ch8.js:493`) even reaches for it — "You wrote it twice: once in the dormitory, when it was a dare, and once just now" — **about WREN, not about the four hands.** The better callback is sitting right next to the one that shipped.

## 5. §C — IS THE REVEAL EARNED?

### 5.1 F3 (four went down, not one): **earned, and well.**

The breadcrumb chain is dense, redundant, multi-channel, and mostly optional in the right way:

| T | breadcrumb | channel | optional? |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | "**four people** closed a wound" — the game's first sentence | Hearth | no |
| T1 | Law 0, struck: "COLD is written by **four hands**" | Binder's Book | no |
| T2 | the lamp lights on **four hands**; "Four hands is how this school does anything that matters" | Hearth | no |
| T4 | four statues, four dials, four holes, none matching | Seer's phone | no |
| T4 | "**four, as one, went through. Not one.**" | Hearth | `CH2_STRIP='right'` |
| T4 | both readings of the strip, in advance | Reader's phone | no |
| T5 | the portraits mutter "**four went down**" | Hearth | no |
| T5 | "four going down the stair and **four coming back**" | Listener's phone | no |
| T6 | four figures under the paint; no child; four shadows | Hearth + art | `TAPESTRY` |
| T7 | four thrones; a Founder's gates that need four readers | Hearth | no |
| T8 | the stone, read from its foot | Hearth | no |

Eleven crumbs across every channel the game has. **Grade: A.** The only defect is §2.1 — the game never lets a character notice the contradiction it opens with — and §2.2, where a design comment assumes an optional crumb.

### 5.2 F5 (it cost them their Sight): **[UNEARNED].**

| T | breadcrumb | channel | optional? |
|---|---|---|---|
| T7 | "**came up grey**" | Reader's Book | **`LETTER` + `JOURNAL`, and delayed one chapter** |
| T7 | a Sighting is spendable and returnable | Hearth | **`STAIR='HOLD'` only** |
| T8 | "Four Masters, four **Sightings**. The Convocation would not pay it." | Binder's Book | **opt-in reveal block** |
| T9 | what *you* lose if you walk | four private phones | `WALK_UNLOCKED` |
| T10 | "**the way the Founders came out: grey-eyed and ordinary**" | Hearth | no |

**Every pre-decision crumb is optional, private, or both.** There is no path on which the shared screen says, before the Decision, that the Founders paid with their Sight. The reveal lands in the epilogue, in a subordinate clause, after the only decision it could have informed.

**[PROPOSED] breadcrumbs, cheapest first — three of these five and the reveal is earned:**

1. **T1, one clause.** The stone's cuts are *burnt*, not worn, and burnt is not the Reader's problem — repairs §13.2 and plants that the fire eats its own record.
2. **T5, the Listener's fine print.** The portraits' *four coming back* gets one more clause: *and all four are painted grey-eyed.* Free; it is a painting; grey eyes are visible; it plants F5 in the one channel that already carries F6.
3. **T6, the tapestry.** The Seer's plate already individuates two figures. Add a third detail: *none of the four has painted eyes* — or, better, *the paint at their eyes is grey and the overpainter did not bother to copy it.* One clause, on the art's own reveal, in the chapter where the picture is the answer.
4. **T7, one whisper at `ch5_descent`.** Marrow names Mere; the Hearth adds *"Reader — you are carrying her handwriting."* Repairs §3.2 and makes the sheet a scene.
5. **T9, Marrow's line at the Decision** (§3.1). The keystone. Without it the other four are foreshadowing with no payoff; with it, the Decision becomes the mystery's climax rather than its epilogue.

### 5.3 F7/F8 (the fire is them; four people is a finite fire): **earned.**

F7 is delivered by the stone the table reads themselves, immediately after a chapter of using Founders' tools. F8 is a single Marrow line and is the game's thesis; the source comment at `ch6.js:840-849` documents why it sits there, and the reasoning holds. **Grade: A−**, docked only because F8 is `CLAIMED BY Marrow` and no other surface corroborates the arithmetic (§13.11 makes the fire's mortality unpriced elsewhere).

### 5.4 F9 (one refused, one never asked): **[WITHHOLDING].**

One sentence, on one phone, on a two-flag path, never referenced again — and the game builds **a door** on it (`ch5.js:287`) that the table can walk through without ever learning what it means. §12.4. The best single ruling available: a fifth person existed and was excluded (§12.4(a)); five arches (§12.26); four thrones; one door. **The arithmetic is already built. Nobody has said the number out loud.**

### 5.5 F15 (who the other three were): **deliberate blank, and it works — with one exception.**

Hooding the statues, effacing the plinth fronts and drawing four identical silhouettes is the mystery's method, not its failure: the Order erased them, and the player feels the erasure. **The exception is Idony** (§4/B9): a Founder whose Law is the mechanism of the Finale and whose name is in a world table nobody reads. Naming her on the rim — "a notch is only a signature" — converts a blank into a *recovered* name, which is a better beat than either alternative.

### The branch-sensitivity headline

**The cost half of this mystery is gated behind `CH2_NICHE` → `LETTER` → `JOURNAL` → `maxChapter>=5` — four gates, three of them player choices, one of them a bug — and sits on one phone at the end of it.** The act half is on eleven surfaces and unmissable. *A table that plays well and chooses "Take it and go" in Chapter II finishes the game never having been told, before the last scene, what the four Founders gave up.*
