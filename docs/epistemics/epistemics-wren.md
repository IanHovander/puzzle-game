## 0. SCOPE

### 0.1 The mystery, stated exactly

**Four impossibilities, one per seat, each privately observed for years and privately explained away
by the observer as their own fault; and the thing those four absences are symptoms of.**

| # | the anomaly | the seat that carries it | the rationalisation, verbatim | cite |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | Wren's name is chalked twice on the dormitory door — once in our letters, once in an alphabet nobody teaches — **and the handwriting is the same** | Reader | "You decided, a year ago, that somebody was being funny. **You have never asked who.**" | `companion/ch0.js:99` |
| **A2** | Has **never once** heard Wren's heart, through anything, anywhere | Listener | "You decided years ago that **the fault was yours**, and you have never said it out loud to anyone." | `companion/ch0.js:112` |
| **A3** | Wren's shadow falls **toward** every fire, including cold ones | Seer | "You decided months ago it was a trick of the light… **It is not the light. It never was.**" | `companion/ch0.js:124` |
| **A4** | **No thread at all**, in either direction — "not unbound. You know unbound." | Binder | "You decided it was a blind spot in your own gift. **You have never told anyone your gift has a blind spot.**" | `companion/ch0.js:145` |

Two further absences accrue and are **never counted as anomalies by anyone**: the memory-bell will
not keep Wren's voice (`companion/ch4.js:227`), and the Cold Ember shares A2 and A4 exactly
(`companion/ch2.js:134-135`, `:165-166`).

**The answer, which the game asserts and never mechanises** (`CANON.md` §4.2, §12.5): Wren is the
hollow — the eighth glyph, COLD, the one glyph with no ladder step and no pitch — wearing a
fourteen-year-old's shape, left on the stones the one night the Hearth was out. The four absences are
one absence seen four ways. It is said out loud exactly once, by Wren, on one ending path:
"It's cold in here. Obviously. **It's me.**" (`ch7.js:697`).

### 0.2 Stakeholders tracked

Wren · Provost Ilsabet Marrow · Lord Cassian Vane · the Reader · the Listener · the Seer · the Binder
· Master Oriel (the only Master with independent knowledge of the cover-up) · Mere (dead; her sheet
is a knower-proxy) · **the real people at the table** (the author asked for them; they are modelled in

stake the game gives them; **the Order/Convocation of 212** is a corporate knower whose only relevant
belief is the cover story it authored, which predates Wren by 386 years.

for it) · **held** (believed, actively not examined) · **suspected** (entertained, unacted-on).

## 12. THE GAP THAT DRIVES THE DRAMA

### 12.1 The pair: **Wren ↔ the Listener**

Of the four asymmetries this mystery contains, three are asymmetries of *fact* and one is an
asymmetry of *blame*. The Reader thinks a joke was played; the Seer thinks a lamp was involved; the
Binder thinks a gift misfired. **Only the Listener has concluded that they are personally defective** —
"You decided years ago that **the fault was yours**, and you have never said it out loud to anyone"
(`companion/ch0.js:112`) — and **Wren has held the exonerating sentence since before the game
started**: "It wasn't a fault in you. **There wasn't one to hear.**" (`companion/ch8.js:94`).

That is the most productive gap in the game because it is the only one where the withholder's silence
*costs the other party something continuously*, in every room, for six chapters, and where the cost is
visible to the player on a phone the withholder cannot see. It also carries the mystery's mechanism:
the Listener's gift is the one that reports *absence as absence* ("Not quiet. **Nothing.**",
`companion/ch5.js:309`), which is precisely what a hollow is.

**Runner-up: Wren ↔ the Seer.** The Seer is the only seat that is asked directly, twice, and can
refuse twice — and the only one Wren thanks "for both" (`companion/ch8.js:112`). It is the cleanest
*choice* in the mystery; the Listener's is the deepest *wound*.

## 13. WHERE THE GAME CONTRADICTS ITSELF ON WHO KNEW WHAT WHEN

### 13.1 **[CONTRADICTION]** Wren has known "for years" about observations that are months old

> `js/content/ch0.js:226` — Wren: "Yes. **All four of you. I've known for years.**"

against the four pages Wren is answering:

> `js/content/companion/ch0.js:99` — Reader: "You decided, **a year ago**, that somebody was being funny."
> `js/content/companion/ch0.js:124` — Seer: "You decided **months ago** it was a trick of the light."

Wren's claim is not "I have had these four properties for years" — the four have just each stated an
*observation*, and Wren answers "**All four of you.** I've known for years", i.e. *I have known that
all four of you noticed*. The Reader's noticing is one year old and the Seer's is months old.

Compounding it, Wren's own T8 callback puts a third figure on the Seer:

> `js/content/ch6.js:329` — LAUNDRY.seer, the non-echo line: "**Six years**, and nobody said it."

is on the four Prologue pages: make the *observation* old and the *rationalisation* recent — "you have

— it is the strongest evidence available that Wren is not bounded like a person — and the game spends
it as small talk.

### (c) Marrow asserts the Order's reading at T3 while able to disprove it at T8

> `js/content/ch1.js:124` — Marrow, to the Convocation: "**The stone over your heads says one born of four.** Tonight I stop arguing and **show you**."

against

> `js/content/ch6.js:824` — "She kneels at the foot, puts one thumb in the first burn, and **reads it the way the Founders cut it.**"
> `js/content/ch7.js:394` — Marrow: "**I scraped it myself, as a girl.**"

She has seen the four figures under the paint, and she can read the stone from the foot. At T3 she
stands under it and tells nine Masters it says *one*. Either (i) she is lying to the Convocation —
defensible, in character, and **marked nowhere**; or (ii) she acquires the foot reading between T3 and
T8, and the game shows no such acquisition. Compounded by §12.42: she says "**show you**" and shows
nothing.

### (d) Marrow disclaims Law 0 at T7 and performs a reading that requires it at T8

> `js/content/ch5.js:399` — Marrow, when the Silent Gate takes COLD: "**That is not in the Book I was given.**"

against

> `js/content/ch6.js:824-828` — she reads the foot: "KNOT · CROWN · ASH · WELL · VEIL · EMBER · **ASH · COLD**"

The Founders' reading writes COLD twice. Law 6 forbids writing it; Law 0 permits it by four hands; Law
3 makes Law 0 bind. Marrow has just said Law 0 is not in her Book. She then reads a sentence whose
last word is COLD, fluently, alone, one chapter later. Either her Book lacks the Law and she reads the
stone anyway (in which case her ch5 surprise is theatre), or she learned Law 0 from the gate one hour
earlier and nothing says so.

### (e) Marrow never once responds to the four anomalies — **[WITHHOLDING]**

She named the child *the hollow of a bell*. She has raised it for fourteen years. She hears all four
anomalies spoken aloud in her presence at `ch6.js:642-678` and her next line is about the fire
(`ch6.js:691`). **The game never establishes whether Marrow knows Wren has no heartbeat, no thread and
a wrong shadow.** That is not mystery, it is a blank: the most interested adult in the building has no
stated position on the mystery's four data points. Two options, both cheap: (i) she knows — one line
at T8 ("I have known since the first night. I could not have you know it") which makes her fourteen
years harder and better; (ii) she does not — one line at T8 (she looks at Wren differently after the
Seer answers) which makes the Second Asking cost her something too.

### (f) The Listener and the Binder are handed the control sample and told not to think

> `js/content/companion/ch2.js:135` — "**The Ember is a stone in a box and you did not expect a heart.**"
> `js/content/companion/ch2.js:166` — "**You have never asked yourself why the two nothings feel different — or whether they are.**"

At T4 two seats hold, on one page each, the identical readings for *a fragment of the Cold* and *Wren*.
Neither draws the inference; one page explicitly forbids it. This is the game's largest deliberate
withhold from its own characters, and it is defensible — but it is also the moment the player is ahead
of both seats, which is fine **once**. Repeated at T6 (the memory-bell), at T7 (the ledge) and at T9
(eight hearts), it stops reading as restraint and starts reading as characters who cannot add up.

### (g) "I have known since the laundry" has no laundry scene — **[UNEARNED]**

> `js/content/ch6.js:689` — Wren: "And — Mum. **I know. I have known since the laundry.**"

`ch3.js:493-530` is the laundry: Bess does not look up, Wren asks four private questions, the four
answer, Wren thanks them. **Nothing in it conveys to Wren what Marrow is or where Wren came from.**
The largest thing Wren learns all night happens off-screen and is reported three chapters later. Two
repairs, both small: (i) put it in the laundry — Bess is sworn to Marrow for thirty years
(`companion/ch3.js:238`) and is the one person in the school who was there fourteen years ago; one
line from the woman at the copper, on the Hearth, costs four words and pays for everything; (ii) or
change Wren's line to "since the study", where the Reader ciphered the name and the Binder read the
grey thread — but then Wren must have learned it from a phone, which breaks the house rule.
**Option (i) is nearly free and is the better story.**

### (h) The Seer is thanked for telling, on every branch, including the one where the Seer did not tell

> `js/content/companion/ch8.js:112` — Wren, to the Seer, **unconditionally**: "**You were the only one who saw it and didn't tell me, and then you did. Thank you for both.**"

`GOODBYE.seer` is a fixed string. A Seer who answered **NOTHING** in the laundry (`WHISPER_seer`) and
**away** at the Second Asking (`ASK_seer`) — i.e. who never told Wren anything — receives a letter
thanking them for telling. The Epilogue asserts a knowledge transfer that the flags say did not
happen. `WHISPER_seer` and `ASK_seer` are both in scope on that page (`ctx.flags`); one conditional
clause fixes it, and the *withheld* variant is the better letter.

### (i) Vane's demand is incoherent with Vane's own belief — **§12.32**

He has known for twenty-two years that the picture says **four, and no child** (`ch7.js:341`). His
entire public argument is that the school's reading is a forgery (`ch1.js:283`). He then demands
**one child** on the strength of a prophecy he has spent two decades calling false, and never once
says what he thinks Wren *is* or what the Crown wants Wren *for*. On E4 the Crown harnesses the Cold
and keeps Wren in a cage — consistent with Wren-as-key, and nobody, including Vane, ever says so.
**The character with the longest-held correct belief in the game never applies it to the child.**
One line at `ch7.js:341` or `ch1.js:136` closes it.

### (j) Nobody in the fiction states the conclusion at T8 — **[WITHHOLDING]**, §12.67

All four anomalies are confirmed aloud, in one room, in five minutes. The stone is read. The word
*hollow* is spoken twice — once as the meaning of Wren's name (`ch6.js:667`) and once as what the fire
is (`ch6.js:828`). **Nobody joins them.** The identification waits for `ch7.js:697`, which is reachable
on one ending. A single line at `ch6_open` — from the Binder's page, or from Wren, or from the
narration that has just said "the fire is only what they left behind" — would let the table *arrive*
at the answer instead of being handed it in the last ten minutes.

### (k) The four never tell each other, or Wren, what they concluded

There is no scene in the game in which the four discuss the four anomalies with each other. T2 speaks
them; T8 speaks them again to Wren. In between, each page restates its own seat's item in isolation,
and the house rule ("**Say what you see. Never show your phone.**", `lore.js:74`) means the *pattern*
exists only at the table, in the real players' heads. **That is a design strength and an in-fiction
hole**: four fourteen-year-olds who shared a room since they were seven, having just discovered they
have each been hiding the same kind of secret, never mention it again for six chapters.
