is unfinished without it.

# 4. THE GAP THAT DRIVES THE DRAMA

## The answer: **Marrow ↔ the four.**

Not Marrow↔Wren (that is the *Wren* mystery's engine, and it is an emotional gap, not an
informational one — by T5 Wren already knows, and Marrow's only remaining secret is that she is
afraid). Not Vane↔Marrow (two people who each know the other knows, in public — superb, and confined
to two scenes). Not Wren↔the four (mutual withholding about Wren, not about the Cold).

**Marrow ↔ the four is the mystery's engine because she holds the complete true account from before
the game and releases it in five instalments, each one exactly as much as the next puzzle needs and
no more — and the last instalment is one the four have to buy with the seal's own currency.**

| instalment | what she gives | what she keeps | cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| T3 | "the Founders left the Cold Ember… if the fire goes out, it lights it again" | that there is a wound; that she can read the stone; that the Ember's function is her word alone | `ch1.js:309` |
| T4 | "that vault was rebuilt once, and **the rebuilding was not honest**" | 212, by name; what was rebuilt over; what the bricked road led to | `ch2.js:189` |
| T6 | "Take Wren down into **the Cold** at midnight, whatever it costs" | what the Cold is | `ch4.js:354` |
| T7 | "Under this school there is a wound. The Founders shut it and left the fire on top. **The fire is going out.**" | that four shut it; that the fire *is* them; the stone | `ch5.js:249-250` |
| T8 | the lid, the push, "It knows", "I can close this wound", and finally **K6** | **that she can read the stone** — until their four readings are spent | `ch6.js:486-488`, `:824`, `:850` |

### Scenes that currently exploit it — and exploit it well

1. **`ch2_start`** (`ch2.js:186-189`). She sends four children under a school to fetch a piece of the
   thing she will not name, and tells them to take the Seer's eyes "because the rebuilding was not
   honest." She points them at the cover-up without naming it. Perfect.
2. **`ch4_marrow`** (`ch4.js:354`). She asks for an oath to take Wren "into the Cold" — a place she
   has defined for nobody — and then says "Read it. **Argue.**" The oath's own text is never printed

3. **`ch5_start`** (`ch5.js:249-250`). Four sentences, and every one of them true. The chapter's whole
   mood turns on the fact that this is the first time.
4. **`ch6_marrow`** (`ch6.js:485-488`). "Miss it and the Cold pushes further. **That is all that
   happens.**" — `CANON.md` calls this "the one wholly honest framing she gives all night" and it is
   correct. It is also the beat where she tells them what the Cold *does* without telling them what
   it *is*.
5. **`ch6_open`** (`ch6.js:850`). K6, in her mouth, at the one beat where the stone has just said
   what the fire is. The chapter's best line.
6. **`ch7_argue1`** (`ch7.js:394-396`). Three consecutive confessions of prior knowledge, each
   triggered by the four naming a thing they found. This is the gap paying out.

# 8. WHERE THE PLAYER LEARNS THE TRUTH, AND WHETHER IT IS EARNED

**The reveal point is T8, `ch6_strip` → `ch6_open`** — the stone read from its foot, immediately
followed by the narration line "**The fire is only what they left behind**" (`ch6.js:839`) and
Marrow's K6 (`:850`). K11 lands one chapter later at T9 (`ch7.js:697`).

**Is it earned? For K1–K10: yes, emphatically, and by an unusually honest breadcrumb ladder.**

| breadcrumb | where | what it plants |
|---|---|---|
| "Four hundred years ago, **four people** closed a wound in the world" | `ch0.js:49` — **line one of the game** | K2, K3 |
| The Order's sentence, immediately after, labelled as **the school's translation** and as a thing every grown-up argues about | `ch0.js:62-63` | that the reading is contested |

| The Ember: no heart, no thread, a shadow that reaches | `companion/ch2.js:134`, `:150`, `:165-166` | K12 — **the Cold and Wren share a signature** |

**Where it is not earned:**

- **K11 is [UNEARNED] as an assertion and earned as an accumulation.** The player has every leg of it
  by T8 and is never given the sentence; then Wren says "It's me" and the room does not react
  (B12, §12.67). The accumulation is excellent. The assertion arrives as a shrug.
- **K16 is never given to anyone, and that is correct** — but §12.1 should be *ruled on* so that the
  game's silences about it are consistent. The art already commits to an answer the prose does not
  (an inverted sky, a cold sun, stars on the roof: `scenes-ch5.js:69-70`).
- **The Great Sigil's gloss is written and withheld** (`ch7.js:130`, §12.24). The table solves an
  eight-word sentence that ends "**the one, and the hollow**" and is never told what it says, in a
  game that printed the Founders' reading from the glosses one chapter earlier. This is the one place
  the mystery's *language* is solved and not translated. A one-clause `solvedText` fixes it.
- **`ch0.js:62`** (X1) is the load-bearing gap: the game's first act of withholding is a claim about
  reading that the game immediately contradicts, about the very sentence the mystery turns on.
